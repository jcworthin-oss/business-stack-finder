Sales and CRM

How to choose a small-business CRM

Choose a CRM based on the sales process your team will actually follow—not on the number of features in a demo.

A CRM becomes valuable when it reliably answers three questions: Which opportunities are active, what must happen next, and who owns the next action? If your team will not maintain those answers, additional automation will not rescue the system.

Match complexity to your sales process

  • Simple relationship selling: contacts, notes, reminders, and a visual pipeline may be enough.
  • Inbound lead generation: prioritize forms, email sequences, source tracking, and response-time automation.
  • Long sales cycles: prioritize custom stages, multiple stakeholders, document history, and forecasting.
  • Account management: prioritize renewals, service records, support handoffs, and customer health.

Calculate the real cost

Include required seats, onboarding, data migration, email volume, calling, automation limits, reporting tiers, and the time required to administer the system. A lower subscription price can still produce a higher operating cost if the team performs duplicate work.

Trial test

Enter ten real opportunities, assign next steps, send a test proposal, and produce a pipeline report. The team should complete those tasks without a consultant or a long internal manual.

Pricing changes frequently. Verify current pricing, plan limits, contracts, integrations, and cancellation terms directly with each vendor.
